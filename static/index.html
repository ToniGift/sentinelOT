A first pass on industrial alerts, done the way a second analyst would

When an OT monitor raises an alarm, the hard part is rarely the alarm. It is working out which machine this is, whether the traffic is normal for it, and what it could do to the process.

SentinelOT does that first pass. It looks the assets up, reads current advisories, maps the activity to MITRE ATT&CK for ICS, and gives you a verdict you can argue with. Every claim points back to a retrieved source or a field in the alert.

Why I built it

I have spent about three years on the plant side of OT security, around PLCs, SCADA and HMIs. On almost every alert, most of the time went on gathering context, not on the decision.

This is my attempt to hand that first half to a machine and keep the judgement with people. Every plant, address and asset on this page is made up.

You are looking at sample data. This page could not reach the SentinelOT API, so only alerts S01 and S06 will run, using saved results. The layout is the real one.

What happens when you press the button

Five model calls and one web search, run in order by plain Python. The small valve symbols are checks written in code: they reject sources that were never retrieved, technique IDs that do not exist, and verdicts that contradict their priority.

Choose an alert above, then run it through these steps.
What each step does
Intake
Reads the alert and the asset record, pulls out addresses, protocols and function codes, and writes up to three search queries.
Tavily search
Runs those queries, trusted domains first (CISA, MITRE ATT&CK, NVD, CVE.org). This step is code, not a model.
Intel
Summarises only what the search returned.
ATT&CK map
Chooses techniques from the official ICS list, or none if nothing fits.
Triage
Decides verdict, priority and confidence, and has to cite its evidence.
Advice
Suggests next steps, each marked as safe to do now or needing an operator's approval.
